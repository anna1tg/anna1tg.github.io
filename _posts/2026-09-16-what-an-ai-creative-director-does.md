Generative tools have made images cheap. Anyone can type a prompt and get something polished in seconds. So what is left for a creative director to do?

Almost everything that matters.

## Strategy comes before the prompt

A good visual starts with a question, not a prompt: *who is this for, and what should they feel?* Before I generate a single frame, I work out the brand's positioning, its audience and the message it needs to carry. That becomes the brief, and the brief is what keeps a hundred generations pointing in one direction.

## Taste is the filter

AI is very good at producing options and very bad at choosing between them. My job is to curate: to see which image fits the brand's character, which one only looks impressive, and which one will still feel right after the tenth time someone sees it.

## Directing, not just generating

The first result is rarely the final one. I refine composition, light, mood and detail across many iterations, combine AI output with traditional editing, and make sure every visual in a campaign looks like it belongs to the same world.

## Where it pays off

- **Brand identity:** a consistent image style that makes a brand recognisable.
- **Ads:** fast concepts and many variations for testing on social platforms.
- **Content:** visuals and short motion pieces for Instagram and TikTok.

## The short version

AI speeds up making. A creative director makes sure you are making the right thing.
